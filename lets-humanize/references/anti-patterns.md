# Anti-Patterns and Repairs

This catalog is diagnostic, not a ban on particular words. A phrase is a problem when it hides meaning, pads the page, overstates evidence, or substitutes a template for judgment. Keep any expression that is precise and useful in its actual context.

## Generic claims

**Signal:** Broad praise or promises with no mechanism, audience, or proof.

**Repair:** Name who benefits, what changes, and what evidence supports the outcome. If no evidence exists, make the statement a proposal rather than a fact.

**Example:** Replace “unlock exponential growth” with a factual description of the action the product enables—only if that capability exists.

## Empty openers and padded endings

**Signal:** A warm-up that delays the answer; a conclusion that repeats every section.

**Repair:** Lead with the answer or decision. End with the final useful implication or next action. Keep context when it changes interpretation.

## Symmetry for symmetry's sake

**Signal:** Three bullets, parallel sentences, or uniform section lengths chosen because the shape feels polished.

**Repair:** Let the number and rhythm follow the actual ideas. A two-part distinction is not improved by inventing a third category.

## Corporate fog

**Signal:** Nouns such as “synergy,” “solution,” “transformation,” or “innovation” obscure the actor and action.

**Repair:** Use a concrete verb and name the object. Keep formal terminology when it has a defined business or technical meaning.

## Artificial casualness

**Signal:** Unrequested slang, intentional typos, jokes, or exaggerated intimacy added to make text seem human.

**Repair:** Match the audience's real relationship and channel. Clear, respectful prose is already human.

## Fake evidence or fake personalization

**Signal:** Invented customers, metrics, quotes, company details, or “I have seen this firsthand” claims.

**Repair:** Remove, verify, or label as hypothetical. Ask for the missing fact only if it materially changes the result.

## Template design

**Signal:** A centered headline plus identical feature cards regardless of product or task.

**Repair:** Rebuild hierarchy from the user's goal and content. Check responsive layout, interaction states, focus, and empty/error cases.

## Over-engineering

**Signal:** New abstractions, dependencies, sections, features, or caveats that do not solve an observed problem.

**Repair:** Keep the smallest change that meets the quality bar; show the cost of anything larger.

## Detector-driven edits

**Signal:** Requests to change perplexity, add random variation, disguise AI use, or guarantee detector outcomes.

**Repair:** Decline the concealment objective. Offer transparent improvements to originality, reasoning, evidence, voice, and readability. Never claim detector certainty.

## A practical test

For every suspicious element, ask: **Which reader need or verified constraint does this serve?** If there is no precise answer, cut it or redesign it. Do not remove necessary legal, safety, accessibility, or technical detail merely to make a piece shorter.
