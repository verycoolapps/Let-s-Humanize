# Research and Strategy Playbook

## Separate evidence from judgment

Tag important statements as one of: **observed evidence**, **interpretation**, **assumption**, or **recommendation**. Preserve source, date, method, and population where they affect meaning. A search result, anecdote, model output, and representative dataset do not support the same strength of claim.

## Build a traceable claim chain

For each material recommendation, make the reasoning inspectable:

**Source or observation → finding → implication → decision → verification metric.**

If a link is missing, say what must be learned. Do not fill the gap with confident language. Distinguish correlation from causation, sample from population, and forecast from measured result. Use ranges or confidence labels when precision is not justified.

## Research hygiene

- Prefer primary and authoritative sources for factual claims.
- Record publication/update date and access date where recency matters.
- Check whether a source measures the same population, geography, period, and outcome as the question.
- Look for contradictory evidence and plausible alternative explanations.
- Minimize personal or sensitive data; use authorized, proportionate collection only.
- Do not invent interviews, survey responses, market sizes, quotations, or citations.

## Recommendations that can be acted on

State the recommended move first, then the evidence and the trade-off. Name the owner or next action only when known; otherwise propose a role, not a fabricated person. Give an observable test and time horizon when the data supports them. If a decision is reversible, say what low-cost experiment would reduce uncertainty. If it is high-stakes or hard to reverse, show the key assumptions and approval needs.

## Useful confidence language

- **High confidence:** multiple relevant, independent sources or strong direct measurement agree.
- **Moderate confidence:** evidence is relevant but limited, indirect, or not fully consistent.
- **Low confidence / hypothesis:** plausible explanation or direction that needs validation.

These labels are qualitative, not statistical probabilities. Do not assign them mechanically; explain the evidence limitation that matters.

## Example

**Weak:** “Customers want an AI assistant, so launch one immediately.”

**Stronger:** “In the supplied support log, repeated questions about order status are common. That suggests an automation opportunity, but it does not establish willingness to use a conversational interface. Prototype order-status self-service for a small, consented cohort; compare completion and escalation rates with the current flow before expanding.”

The stronger recommendation keeps the evidence boundary visible and names a test. Do not use it as a real finding unless the supplied log actually supports it.

## Exercise

Choose a recommendation from a strategy draft. Trace it backward to its sources and forward to a measurable outcome. Mark missing links as assumptions. Rewrite the recommendation so a skeptical reviewer can tell what is known, what is inferred, and what the proposed test would teach.
